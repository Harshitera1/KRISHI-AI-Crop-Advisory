Fertilizer timing should follow crop demand, root activity, rainfall risk, and soil test results instead of a fixed calendar alone. Apply phosphorus and potassium before planting or near the root zone when the test calls for them. Split nitrogen between planting, active vegetative growth, and the crop's highest-demand stage to reduce leaching and lodging. Avoid spreading fertilizer immediately before heavy rain or onto saturated soil. Keep granular fertilizer away from seed and stems unless the label allows contact. On sandy soils, smaller split applications are often more efficient than one large dose. Irrigate appropriately after application, but avoid runoff. Record product, rate, date, field, and crop stage. Compare plant color and growth with untreated strips when possible. A balanced nutrient plan is safer than correcting one suspected deficiency with an excessive amount.
