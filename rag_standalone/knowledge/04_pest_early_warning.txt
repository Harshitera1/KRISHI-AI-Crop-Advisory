Scout fields at least twice each week, especially after warm, humid, or windy weather. Early pest warnings include fresh holes, chewed leaf margins, leaf mines, sticky honeydew, webbing, curled leaves, wilted shoots, frass, and clusters of eggs or young larvae on leaf undersides. Inspect both healthy and affected plants and walk field borders as well as the center. Record the pest, location, crop stage, and number of affected plants so changes can be measured. Yellowing alone does not prove insect damage; nutrient deficiency, disease, drought, and root stress may cause similar symptoms. Remove heavily infested leaves when practical and protect beneficial insects. Use traps or scouting counts to decide whether treatment is justified. Identify the pest before selecting a crop-labeled product, follow the label, and rotate modes of action to reduce resistance.
