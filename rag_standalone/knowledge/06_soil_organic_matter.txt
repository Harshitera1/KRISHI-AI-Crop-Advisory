Soil organic matter improves aggregation, water infiltration, nutrient cycling, and biological activity. Build it gradually by returning healthy crop residues, adding mature compost, using suitable manure, and planting cover crops that fit the local climate. Keep living roots or residue on the soil as long as practical and reduce unnecessary tillage, which can accelerate decomposition and erosion. Avoid applying unfinished compost or excessive manure because salts, weed seeds, and nutrient runoff can damage crops. Prevent compaction by limiting traffic on wet fields and maintaining drainage. Rotate crops so residues and root systems vary across seasons. Test organic matter periodically using the same sampling depth and locations so trends can be compared. Organic matter does not replace a soil test or immediately correct pH and nutrient shortages. Use measured amendments and allow several seasons for soil structure and water-holding improvements to become clear.
