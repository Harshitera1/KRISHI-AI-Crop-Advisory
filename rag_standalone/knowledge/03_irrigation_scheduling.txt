Irrigation timing should match crop growth stage, soil texture, weather, and root depth. Water early in the morning when possible to reduce evaporation and give foliage time to dry. Wheat commonly needs reliable moisture around crown-root initiation, flowering, and grain filling. Rice uses managed flooding or saturated soil according to its growth stage; avoid uncontrolled standing water when drainage is poor. Maize needs particular attention around tasseling, silking, and grain development. Vegetables and fruiting crops need steady moisture during flowering and fruit set. Check soil moisture below the surface before watering instead of relying only on a dry top layer. Deep, less frequent irrigation is often better than shallow daily watering, but sandy soils may need smaller, more frequent applications. Stop or reduce irrigation after substantial rain, and improve drainage where water remains ponded.
