When a crop is stressed, diagnose the whole field before treating it. Compare symptoms on old and new leaves, inspect roots, note recent weather, and check irrigation uniformity, drainage, compaction, salinity, and herbicide exposure. Mark affected patches and photograph symptoms with the date and crop stage. Take a representative soil sample from the root zone, avoiding unusual spots such as manure piles or field edges. If a disease or pest is suspected, use a local agricultural laboratory or extension service for confirmation. Do not tank-mix several products without checking labels and compatibility. Small, targeted changes followed by a short recheck are safer than repeated broad applications. Keep a field log so recurring patterns can be linked to soil, weather, variety, and management decisions. Confirm fertilizer rates with a soil test, and follow all product safety, harvest interval, and protective equipment requirements.
