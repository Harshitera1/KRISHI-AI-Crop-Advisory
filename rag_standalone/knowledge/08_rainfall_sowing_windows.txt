Rainfall should guide sowing decisions, but one storm is not enough evidence of a safe planting window. Check the local forecast, recent rainfall, soil moisture below the surface, and the expected rainfall pattern for the next two weeks. For rainfed crops, sow after the soil has received enough steady moisture for germination and when heavy runoff is unlikely. Avoid sowing into a shallow wet layer over dry soil because seedlings may emerge unevenly and fail during the next dry period. Delay field traffic and planting when soil is sticky to prevent compaction. If heavy rain is forecast immediately after sowing, consider erosion risk, seed displacement, and drainage. Match the crop and variety to the reliable length of the local rainy season. Keep a backup plan for re-sowing, supplemental irrigation, or a shorter-duration crop when rainfall arrives late or stops early.
