Nutrient deficiencies often appear first in leaves. Nitrogen deficiency causes uniform pale green or yellow older leaves, slow growth, and thin stems. Phosphorus deficiency can cause slow root development, dark green foliage, or purple coloring in cool conditions. Potassium deficiency commonly causes yellow or scorched leaf edges, weak stems, and poor drought tolerance. Confirm symptoms with a representative soil test because drought, disease, root damage, and compaction can look similar. Apply only the nutrient shown to be low, using the soil laboratory rate and a crop-appropriate product. Split nitrogen applications during active growth to reduce leaching. Place phosphorus near the root zone without touching seed or roots. Apply potassium according to soil texture and crop removal, especially in sandy fields. Recheck plant growth after two weeks and record the product, rate, date, and field response.
