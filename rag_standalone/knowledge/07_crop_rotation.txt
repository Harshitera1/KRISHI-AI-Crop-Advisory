Crop rotation means growing different crop families in sequence on the same field. A useful rotation interrupts pest and disease life cycles, varies rooting depth, spreads labor and nutrient demand, and improves residue diversity. Include a legume such as chickpea, lentil, or mungbean when it suits the climate and market; legumes can contribute nitrogen through root symbiosis, but they do not supply unlimited nitrogen to the next crop. Avoid planting the same family repeatedly, such as cereal after cereal or a vegetable after a related vegetable, when shared pests and diseases are common. Pair deep-rooted and shallow-rooted crops to explore different soil layers. Keep records of crop, variety, planting date, inputs, weeds, pests, and yield. Design rotations around water availability and local sowing windows, and use cover crops where they will not exhaust stored moisture before the next cash crop.
