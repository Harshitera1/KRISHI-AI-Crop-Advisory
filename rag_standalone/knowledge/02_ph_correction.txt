Soil pH controls how available nutrients are to crop roots. Test several root-zone samples from each uniform field area before applying an amendment; one handful may not represent the field. Acidic soils can usually be corrected gradually with agricultural lime, but the amount depends on soil texture, buffering capacity, and the target crop. Incorporate lime where practical and allow time for it to react. Alkaline soils may respond to elemental sulfur, organic matter, and improved drainage, but sulfur works slowly and should follow a laboratory recommendation. Do not use household acids or excessive amendments as quick fixes. Keep pH records and retest on a regular schedule. Organic matter supports nutrient cycling but does not instantly replace a pH correction plan. Select the target range for the crop, because a pH suitable for one crop may not be ideal for another.
